Friction Heatmap
Sources and types of customer friction. Understanding what's structural vs controllable.
🟥 Structural Friction
(3 constraints)
Imposed by external factors (law, physics). Cannot eliminate, must design around.
Physical vehicle inspection required
In-person signature (state law)
DMV lien release (7-14 days)
🟧 Policy Friction
(5 constraints)
Avg Complexity:
TFS business rules and policy choices. Could change with business justification.
HI/AK: Ship vehicle (not disclosed upfront)
NH/WI: Additional $150 fee (surprise charge)
Non-originating dealer: Approval call required
Early termination: Fee negotiation (no transparency)
Security deposit: 7-14 day mail delay
🟨 Design Friction
(5 gaps)
Avg Complexity:
UX gaps and missing features. Pure execution problem - should not exist.
Inspection scheduling: Phone-only (no online)
Report delivery: Mail (no digital tracking)
Dealer coordination: Customer must initiate
Invoice arrival: 60-120 day opacity
AutoPay cancellation: Separate forgotten step
Key Insight
77% of friction points are policy or design choices - within TFS control to address. Only 3 constraints are truly structural. The majority of customer pain is self-inflicted and improvable.

Customer-as-Integrator Problem
27% of journeys require customers to coordinate 3+ organizational parties. Digital platform gaps (42% vs 75% benchmark) force customers into Contact Center (28% ownership), which then deflects to Dealer (15% with high variability). Customer absorbs coordination cost, time delays, and context loss at each handoff.
Handoff Impact on Burden
Average parties per journey: 3.2 (vs 2.0-2.5 benchmark)
Customer-as-Integrator journeys: 27% (34 journeys require 3+ party coordination)
Digital Platform Gap

Best-in-class: 75%+

  • 22% of contact center volume is avoidable
  • Customers forced into 28% higher-burden channels
  • Context loss at each digital→offline transition
